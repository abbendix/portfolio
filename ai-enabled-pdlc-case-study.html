The leadership question

How can AI shorten low-value production work while increasing the time and evidence available for difficult product decisions?

AI acceleratesSynthesis, structure, drafting, prototyping and repeatable reporting.
The PM decidesOutcomes, priorities, trade-offs, evidence thresholds and what ships.
Trust is designed inSources, explicit uncertainty, review gates and measurable adoption.

The opportunity

Product work loses time at the seams.

Evidence, strategy, designs, requirements, tickets and delivery signals often live in different tools. Teams spend time recreating context, formatting outputs and translating between disciplines.

01Fragmented context

Customer research, analytics, strategy and technical constraints are difficult to hold together when a decision is made.

02Repeated translation

The same intent is rewritten into briefs, PRDs, prototypes, tickets, updates and decision papers—creating delay and drift.

03Unstructured AI use

Individual prompting creates inconsistent outputs, weak traceability and demonstrations that never become everyday practice.

My product hypothesis: AI creates durable value when it connects trusted context to reusable workflows and explicit human decision points—not when it simply generates more content.

Discover + define

Use AI to widen the evidence base—not to choose the strategy.

Input → decisionResearch, analytics, strategy and constraints become a reviewable problem frame.

What AI helps me do

  • Structure and compare research, feedback and performance signals.
  • Identify themes, contradictions, evidence gaps and assumptions.
  • Draft opportunity statements, outcome trees and discovery briefs.
  • Carry agreed strategy and terminology into later artefacts.

What I remain accountable for

  • Deciding which customer and business problem deserves investment.
  • Judging evidence quality, relevance and uncertainty.
  • Choosing outcomes and success measures.
  • Aligning stakeholders around what will not be pursued.
  1. Ground the workspaceConnect current strategy, product context and validated source material.
  2. Ask for gaps and tensionsUse AI to challenge completeness rather than merely confirm the initial idea.
  3. Approve the problem frameA human-reviewed opportunity and outcome becomes the input to solution work.

Design + validate

Move from words to testable experiences faster.

Options, not answersAI increases solution breadth; customers and evidence determine direction.

Prototype

Create realistic HTML concepts using known patterns and content, making abstract requirements tangible earlier.

Pressure-test

Surface edge cases, accessibility risks, failure states, data needs and unintended consequences before build.

Experiment

Translate risky assumptions into hypotheses, control/variant definitions, metrics and guardrails.

Example: in the identity case, the migration direction is fixed, but the login experience is not assumed. A controlled experiment measures login success and downstream conversion before wider rollout.

Deliver + learn

Keep product intent visible through execution.

Traceable deliveryThe reason for the work travels with the requirements, tickets and measures.

Delivery workflow

  • Turn approved requirements into scoped Jira tickets and acceptance criteria.
  • Expose dependencies, open decisions, risks and ownership gaps.
  • Reuse terminology and evidence rather than reconstructing context.
  • Prepare stakeholder updates from current delivery signals.

Learning workflow

  • Structure qualitative feedback into themes and actionable product inputs.
  • Compare observed results with the original hypothesis and guardrails.
  • Maintain a decision log with evidence and unresolved uncertainty.
  • Feed learning into the next discovery cycle.

Human in the loop

Acceleration without abdication.

The model assigns work to AI where speed and consistency help, while reserving judgement, accountability and sensitive decisions for people.

AI can propose and produce

  • Synthesis and structured first drafts
  • Alternative framings and edge cases
  • Prototype code and documentation
  • Repeatable transformations between artefacts
  • Validation checks for consistency and completeness

The product leader must decide

  • Which outcomes matter and why
  • Whether evidence is sufficient and trustworthy
  • Which trade-offs are acceptable
  • What is safe, viable and strategically aligned
  • What ships—and accountability for the result

Proof in practice

This portfolio was built through the same lifecycle.

The collaboration with Kiro is a small but transparent example of the method: grounded inputs, iterative decisions, explicit corrections and validated outputs.

Evidence and human direction

  • CV and drafted case studies
  • Career facts, product context and target positioning
  • Corrections on ownership, terminology and confirmed metrics
  • Explicit instruction not to invent evidence

AI-supported work and validated outputs

  • Identified inconsistent 21M/22M figures and aligned the confirmed milestone.
  • Separated an 80% reuse target from an achieved result.
  • Kept the identity experiment provisional and stated clearly that the provider migration is not complete.
  • Distinguished workflows I developed from shared-skill rollout I supported.
  • Produced standard and vertical HTML portfolios, then checked structure, links and claims.
The important point: AI did not decide which claims were true. It surfaced inconsistencies, generated options and implemented approved changes; I supplied corrections and remained accountable for the final narrative.

Current evidence

What I can claim today—and what still needs measurement.

I separate demonstrated activity and adoption examples from future impact claims.

Demonstrated

  • Developed and applied three repeatable workflows: PM companion, feedback analysis and delivery reporting.
  • Built a Design System MCP server connecting AI tools to trusted components, tokens and guidance.
  • Supported rollout of reusable skills for discovery, PRDs, Jira tickets and prototyping.
  • Hosted the Nordic Agentathon and contributed to AI-enabled lifecycle advisory work.

Not claimed without evidence

  • Percentage reduction in PRD or ticket-production time.
  • Number of active teams or repeat users.
  • Quality improvement attributable to AI.
  • Commercial or customer impact from AI-enabled delivery.
Trust principle: two concrete examples of recurring use are stronger than an impressive percentage with no baseline, denominator or dated source.

Measurement plan

Measure whether the lifecycle improves—not whether AI generates more.

The goal is better product decisions and faster learning. Output volume alone is not success.

Cycle timeTime from evidence to approved brief, PRD, prototype or build-ready story.
ReworkRevision rounds caused by missing context, ambiguity or inconsistent requirements.
AdoptionActive teams, repeat users and recurring workflow runs—not one-time trials.
QualityHuman review scores, acceptance rates, traceability and defects found before delivery.
Learning speedTime from assumption to customer or behavioural evidence and a recorded decision.
Next evidence step: baseline two repeatable workflows, sample comparable tasks, record review effort and monitor repeat usage over time.

Leadership takeaway

AI changes the product system—not the accountability.

My role is to connect trusted context, reusable workflows and human judgement so teams spend less time recreating artefacts and more time understanding customers, making decisions and learning from outcomes.

Build reusable capabilityTurn successful experiments into repeatable workflows and shared context.
Design for trustMake evidence, uncertainty, ownership and approval visible at every stage.
Optimise for learningJudge the system by decision quality, adoption and outcome speed—not generated volume.

“Use AI to accelerate the work around product judgement—so people can spend more time exercising it.”
