01 · Platform strategy · Scale · Commercial transformation

From local accounts to a global customer platform

21M accounts in three years · 15+ markets and brands · one shared platform

The situation

Customer-account experiences were fragmented across markets and dependent on legacy platforms. Launching for another market or brand required significant local implementation, increasing cost and lead time. The account was also treated mainly as a post-booking service destination, despite its potential to support recognition, loyalty, engagement and incremental sales.

My mandate

As Global Senior Product Owner, I led the Customer Account domain across TUI markets and sub-brands. I owned the product direction, roadmap and cross-market rollout, translating the global platform strategy into a sequenced backlog that delivered customer value while reducing legacy dependency.

The decisions I drove

  • Build a shared foundation before adding local features. I established authentication, profile and booking access as reusable capabilities on a common codebase.
  • Standardise before scaling. I kept Authentication, Profile and Bookings as global capabilities, while allowing consent and marketing-permission rules to vary where local regulation or market propositions required it. This protected reuse without forcing markets into non-compliant or irrelevant customer experiences.
  • Turn account into a commercial channel. I prioritised consent and marketing permissions, notifications, trip-planning tools, offers and ancillary upsell after the trusted core was in place.
  • Use migration to simplify the estate. I connected market rollout to the retirement of UK customer-data, Nordic and other legacy account services.

How I led

I aligned product, engineering, design, security, commercial and market stakeholders around one roadmap. The rollout reached more than 15 markets and brands, including UK & Ireland, German-speaking markets, Benelux, the Nordics, Musement and Tours. I balanced shared product outcomes with regulatory, customer and operational needs in each market.

Outcome

  • Scaled the global Customer Account platform to 21 million accounts within three years, achieving the global KPI target.
  • Established Authentication, Profile and Bookings as reusable foundations on one shared codebase.
  • Delivered major account and identity migrations while enabling legacy decommissioning.
  • Established shared customer capabilities including Notifications, Trip-Planning Toolbox, Deals & Offers, consent management and ancillary upsell.
  • Received a senior-leadership Certificate of Excellence in 2025 for outstanding contribution to global e-commerce.

What this demonstrates

Global platform product leadership: setting a long-term product direction, balancing standardisation with market needs, and sequencing technical foundations, customer value and commercial outcomes across a complex organisation.

02 · Ongoing transformation · Risk management · Experimentation

De-risking an identity transformation

Migration in progress · provisional experiment evidence · decision before scale

The situation

TUI is migrating customer identity from SAP Gigya to Ping Identity. This is not only a technical replacement: login sits across every authenticated journey, and failure could reduce successful sign-ins, disrupt booking access and affect downstream conversion.

My mandate

After returning to Customer Account in August 2026, I took ownership of the web-login product definition within the ongoing identity transformation. My role is to help keep migration progress moving while coordinating identity architecture, customer experience, security, web delivery and the brand refresh.

The decisions I drove

  • Defined the customer journey, not just the integration. I wrote the PRD and commissioned end-to-end UX discovery covering entry points, redirects, recovery, errors, session continuity and return to the original journey.
  • Protected the critical path. I removed an advanced fraud-protection capability from the first release when it threatened delivery, preserving it as a follow-on capability rather than allowing uncontrolled scope growth.
  • Tested the riskiest UX assumption. Instead of treating a full-page redirect as an unavoidable technical choice, I established a controlled comparison with the existing login drawer across two market regions.
  • Agreed decision criteria upfront. Login success and downstream funnel conversion became the decision metrics, ensuring the team would not optimise for technical completion at the expense of customer and business performance.

How I am leading

I established a weekly cross-team delivery forum, mapped programme dependencies and connected technical milestones to measurable customer outcomes. The experiment launched on 29 October 2026 with estimated monthly exposure of approximately 75,000 users.

Provisional outcome — experiment and migration in progress

  • The live experiment is generating behavioural evidence to inform the login-experience decision; analysis is not yet final.
  • The first-release scope separates essential migration capabilities from deferrable fraud-protection functionality.
  • Cross-team dependencies across identity, web, design and adjacent platforms are now visible through a shared delivery forum.
  • The identity-provider migration is not complete. No login or conversion improvement is claimed until the experiment closes and the wider migration has been delivered and measured.

What this demonstrates

Product judgement under risk: framing infrastructure as a customer and business problem, making deliberate scope trade-offs and using experimentation before an irreversible platform decision.

03 · Platform adoption · Delivery leadership · Organisational change

Making the TUI Design System adoptable

Six-month leadership assignment · TUI Design System · brand-refresh foundation

The situation

TUI was preparing a company-wide brand refresh across digital channels. Without a themeable shared component library, teams would have needed to update similar interfaces independently—creating duplicated effort, inconsistent customer experiences and delivery risk.

My mandate

I was seconded to the Inspire product area for six months to lead delivery of two distinct platform products: the TUI Design System and the Static Site Generation and routing platform. My task was to maintain delivery across separate teams while making the TUI Design System ready for organisation-wide adoption.

The decisions I drove

  • Prioritised leverage over component volume. I put theming and design-to-code tooling, including Figma Code Connect, ahead of lower-leverage additions because they reduced rework across consuming teams.
  • Treated adoption as part of the product. I introduced workshops, an adoption tracker and readiness reviews rather than assuming teams would migrate simply because the system existed.
  • Made cross-team risk visible. Shared milestones and senior-leadership reporting exposed blockers, ownership and readiness across product areas.
  • Connected trusted product knowledge to AI. I delivered the first TUI Design System MCP server within Digital Sales Solutions, making components, tokens and guidance available to developers, designers and AI agents.

How I led

I partnered with design leadership, front-end engineering and product teams across the organisation. Readiness reviews converted a broad transformation into explicit commitments: what each team needed to change, who owned it and when it would be ready.

Outcome

  • Delivered the TUI Design System through its UI Library v5 release, creating a production-ready and themeable foundation for shared digital products.
  • Created a central technical enabler for applying the brand refresh consistently across digital channels.
  • Introduced an adoption model that gave leadership one view of readiness and made blockers actionable.
  • Connected the TUI Design System to AI-supported development through an MCP server.

What this demonstrates

Adoption-led platform thinking: a shared product creates value only when consuming teams can migrate, making enablement, governance, measurement and accountability part of the roadmap.

04 · AI enablement · Product operations · Innovation

Turning AI interest into reusable product practice

AI Champion · three applied workflows · product-community enablement

The situation

Product teams were exploring AI individually, but experimentation was fragmented. Colleagues repeatedly recreated prompts and workflows, while general-purpose tools lacked access to trusted company context, product practices and team knowledge.

My mandate

I set out to turn emerging AI capability into practical and repeatable product work. I personally developed and applied workflows in my own product area, while supporting the wider rollout of reusable skills and AI-enabled ways of working across the product community.

The decisions I drove

  • Prove value through everyday work. I developed three practical workflows: a PM companion, a customer-feedback analyst and automated weekly delivery reporting.
  • Support repeatable product methods. I helped roll out reusable skills for discovery, PRD development, Jira ticket creation and prototyping so Product Managers could begin with shared guidance rather than a blank prompt.
  • Connect agents to trusted knowledge. I built a TUI Design System MCP server that made current components, tokens and guidance available to compatible AI tools.
  • Pair tools with enablement. I combined hands-on use cases with demonstrations, training and facilitated events because sustainable adoption required both confidence and immediately useful assets.
  • Influence the operating model. I contribute to the Agentic Advisory Group, helping shape an AI-enabled product-development lifecycle and proof of concept.

Adoption in practice

  • Recurring delivery reporting: I moved weekly delivery-report preparation in my product area into a repeatable AI-supported workflow rather than leaving it as a one-off experiment.
  • Customer insight: I applied an AI-supported feedback-analysis workflow to structure customer comments into themes and usable product inputs.
  • Product-manager enablement: shared skills and practical use cases became material for demonstrations, training and hands-on sessions with the product community.

How I led

I hosted the Nordic Agentathon in September 2026, ran demonstrations and share-and-learn sessions, and supported AI enablement for Product Managers and Digital Sales Solutions colleagues. I was selected as a TUI AI Champion through a separate application process.

Outcome to date

  • Developed and applied three repeatable workflows spanning product support, customer insight and delivery reporting.
  • Supported the rollout of reusable skills across discovery, requirements, ticket creation and prototyping.
  • Connected trusted design-system knowledge to AI-supported development through an MCP server.
  • Created a bridge between hands-on product practice and the advisory work shaping TUI’s future product-development lifecycle.

What this demonstrates

Applied AI product leadership: translating emerging technology into repeatable practice, building organisational confidence and distinguishing directly owned delivery from wider rollout and enablement.

Product leadership approach

How I lead products

My experience comes from travel, but my approach applies wherever complex customer journeys, technology platforms and organisational scale meet.

  • Start with customer and business outcomes. I combine qualitative insight, behavioural data and commercial context to define the problem before committing to a solution.
  • Build reusable foundations. I look for capabilities, interfaces and standards that solve today’s need while reducing the cost of future change.
  • Make risk visible and testable. I surface assumptions, define decision criteria and use experiments or incremental delivery before irreversible investments.
  • Treat adoption as a product outcome. I pair delivery with enablement, governance and measurement so shared capabilities create value in real teams and customer journeys.
  • Connect disciplines around decisions. I bring product, design, engineering, data, security and commercial stakeholders together around explicit trade-offs and accountable outcomes.

The consistent thread: turn complexity into clear product decisions, then build the alignment and reusable systems needed to deliver at scale.
