01 · Build and scale platforms

From local accounts to a global customer platform

22M in 3 yearsAcross 15+ markets and brands on one shared platform

The situation

Customer-account experiences were fragmented across markets and dependent on legacy platforms. Local launches added cost and lead time, while the account was treated mainly as a service destination rather than a recognition, loyalty and commercial channel.

My mandate

As Global Senior Product Owner, I led the product direction, roadmap and cross-market rollout—turning a global platform strategy into a sequenced backlog that delivered customer value while reducing legacy dependency.

  • Build the foundation firstEstablish Authentication, Profile and Bookings as reusable capabilities before adding local features.
  • Standardise with deliberate exceptionsKeep core capabilities global while allowing consent and marketing-permission rules to vary for regulation or market propositions.
  • Turn account into a commercial channelAdd notifications, trip planning, offers and ancillary opportunities after the trusted core was established.
  • Use migration to simplifyConnect rollout to retirement of UK customer-data, Nordic and other legacy account services.

01 · Outcomes

Scale came from protecting the common core.

The rollout reached UK & Ireland, German-speaking markets, Benelux, the Nordics, Musement and Tours—while preserving one shared product direction.

  • Scaled the platform to 22 million accounts within three years, achieving the global KPI target.
  • Reached more than 15 markets and brands through reusable account foundations.
  • Delivered major account and identity migrations while enabling legacy decommissioning.
  • Established Notifications, Trip-Planning Toolbox, Deals & Offers, consent management and ancillary upsell as shared customer capabilities.
  • Received a senior-leadership Certificate of Excellence in 2025 for outstanding contribution to global e-commerce.

“Standardise what creates leverage. Localise only where customer, regulatory or commercial value justifies the cost.”

02 · Make difficult product decisions

De-risking an identity transformation

~75k users/monthEstimated experiment exposure; analysis remains provisional
Migration and experiment in progress

The situation

TUI is migrating customer identity from SAP Gigya to Ping Identity. Login spans every authenticated journey, so a poor transition could reduce successful sign-ins, disrupt booking access and affect downstream conversion.

My mandate

After returning to Customer Account in August 2026, I took ownership of web-login product definition within the ongoing transformation, coordinating customer experience, identity architecture, security and web delivery.

  • Define the journey, not just the integrationCover entry points, redirects, recovery, errors, session continuity and return to the original journey.
  • Protect the critical pathMove advanced fraud protection out of the first release when it threatened delivery, preserving it as follow-on capability.
  • Test the riskiest UX assumptionCompare the full-page redirect with the existing login drawer across two market regions.
  • Agree criteria before seeing resultsUse login success and downstream conversion so technical completion cannot mask customer harm.

02 · Provisional outcomes

Evidence before an irreversible platform decision.

I established a weekly cross-team delivery forum and connected technical milestones to measurable customer outcomes. The experiment launched on 29 October 2026.

No uplift claimed — analysis not final
  • The live experiment is generating behavioural evidence to inform the login-experience decision.
  • The first-release scope separates essential migration capabilities from deferrable fraud-protection functionality.
  • Dependencies across identity, web, design and adjacent platforms are visible through a shared delivery forum.
  • The provider migration is not complete. Login and conversion improvements will only be claimed after delivery and measurement.
Leadership signal: frame infrastructure as a customer and business problem, make scope trade-offs explicit and test before scaling.

03 · Drive organisational adoption

Making the TUI Design System adoptable

6-month assignmentTwo platform products, separate teams, organisation-wide dependencies

The situation

A company-wide brand refresh needed to reach multiple digital channels. Without a themeable shared component library, teams would repeat the same work and create inconsistent customer experiences.

My mandate

I was seconded to lead delivery of the TUI Design System and the Static Site Generation and routing platform, maintaining two distinct product backlogs while preparing the design system for broad adoption.

  • Prioritise leverage over component volumePut theming and Figma Code Connect ahead of lower-leverage additions.
  • Treat adoption as product workIntroduce workshops, an adoption tracker and readiness reviews instead of assuming teams would migrate.
  • Make cross-team risk visibleUse shared milestones and leadership reporting to expose blockers, owners and readiness.
  • Connect trusted knowledge to AIBuild a Design System MCP server exposing components, tokens and guidance to compatible AI tools.

03 · Outcomes

A platform is successful when other teams can use it.

The product was the TUI Design System; UI Library v5 was the production release through which it was delivered.

  • Delivered a production-ready, themeable foundation for shared digital products through UI Library v5.
  • Created a central technical enabler for applying the brand refresh consistently across channels.
  • Introduced an adoption model giving leadership one view of readiness and actionable blockers.
  • Connected trusted design-system knowledge to AI-supported development through an MCP server.

“Shared capabilities create value through adoption—not through the number of components shipped.”

04 · Shape future product development

Turning AI interest into reusable product practice

3 applied workflowsProduct support, customer insight and delivery reporting

The situation

Product teams were experimenting independently. Colleagues recreated prompts and workflows, while general-purpose tools lacked trusted company context, product practices and team knowledge.

My mandate

I developed and applied workflows in my own product area while supporting wider rollout of reusable skills and AI-enabled ways of working across the product community.

  • Prove value through everyday workDevelop a PM companion, customer-feedback analyst and automated weekly delivery reporting.
  • Support repeatable methodsHelp roll out shared skills for discovery, PRDs, Jira ticket creation and prototyping.
  • Connect agents to trusted contextUse an MCP server to make current design-system knowledge queryable.
  • Pair tooling with enablementCombine practical assets with training, demonstrations and facilitated events.

04 · Adoption in practice

From demonstrations to repeatable work.

No time-saving or adoption percentage is claimed without a measured baseline. The evidence here is concrete use, reusable assets and organisational enablement.

Recurring delivery reporting

Moved weekly delivery-report preparation in my product area into a repeatable AI-supported workflow.

Customer insight

Applied an AI-supported analysis workflow to structure customer comments into themes and usable product inputs.

Product-community enablement

Used shared skills and practical examples in demonstrations, training and hands-on sessions.

  • Developed and applied three repeatable workflows across product support, insight and reporting.
  • Supported rollout of reusable skills across discovery, requirements, tickets and prototyping.
  • Hosted the Nordic Agentathon and was selected as a TUI AI Champion.
  • Connected hands-on product practice to the advisory work shaping an AI-enabled product-development lifecycle.
Ownership distinction: I developed and applied the three workflows; I supported the broader rollout of shared skills.
